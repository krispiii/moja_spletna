SIMOPS

About the project

A step-by-step instructor tool for configuring and launching military simulation exercises. The operator moves through: environment, mission type, simulator assignment, soldier placement, equipment loadout, tracking settings, review & launch. Each step validates inputs and blocks progression on critical errors. Smart defaults keep setup fast; a final overview screen shows everything before the exercise goes live.

  • Industry

    Simulation training

    An industry that uses software and hardware systems to replicate real-world scenarios replacing costly and resource-heavy live exercises with controlled, repeatable practice environments. Trainees can rehearse complex situations, build muscle memory and be evaluated all without the risks or logistics of the real thing.

  • The problem

    Fast setup, clear choices

    Instructors need to set up exercises quickly, but each configuration step carries real consequences every choice has limitations, affects what’s possible next and must be clearly understood before moving on. The challenge is designing a flow that is both fast and informed: operators should never feel lost about what they’re selecting, what’s off the table, or how their decisions shape the rest of the setup.

  • User groups

    Simulation instructors

    Instructors who plan and manage simulation training exercises. They are familiar with the equipment and scenarios but work under time pressure often configuring multiple sessions in a single day. They need the system to be efficient and unambiguous, not hand-holding.

Design process

The first step was mapping the full configuration flow as a decision tree each step branches into its possibilities, and each branch connects to the next. Every choice a user makes directly affects what’s available in the steps that follow, making dependencies visible rather than hidden. The visual structure shows all options without overwhelming enough information to decide confidently, nothing more.

How it is used

The setup is broken into individual steps, each presented one at a time. The instructor makes a selection and moves forward or goes back to change a previous choice at any point. Depending on what was selected earlier, some options in later steps may be limited or unavailable. Nothing is locked away permanently, the flow stays flexible while keeping the path clear.

Conclusion

The goal was to design a setup flow that respects the instructor’s time without sacrificing clarity. Every step is intentional, every decision visible and the path from configuration to launch is as short and confident as possible.
